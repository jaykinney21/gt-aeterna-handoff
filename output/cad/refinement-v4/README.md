# Every Step Legacy — source-traced appearance v4

This rebuild follows document 63's original reference and the owner's correction list. It preserves v3 and the separate engineering study. It is an **appearance model**, with no engineered cavity, capacity, closure or production-readiness claim.

## What changed

- Two independently traced, full-height curves now define the sculpture. Bronze follows the first curve. The broad black blade lies between that curve and the separate second black rim, which wraps around the rear. The smooth writing shell sits to the right/below the second curve.
- The source front boundary landmarks are recorded in `appearance-status.json` and the Python source. Approximate 2D reference coordinates guide the front composition; hidden depths remain an interpretation.
- The tilted oval top has one closed planar dark textured face and a bronze perimeter. Its geometry is not an opening or approved removable closure.
- Body texture uses dense irregular shallow nubbles instead of a uniform metallic stud grid. Front stitching is a fine connected two-rail ladder. The rear does not duplicate that front stitch feature.
- Base proportions return to approximately 171.45 mm diameter and 50.8 mm height. Three dark textured bands have rounded nodules, with dominant bronze rims only at the top and bottom. The exposed top annulus is textured.
- Front and rear plaques are wide, low and bronze-framed. Texture is excluded behind plaque faces. Rear name/date text remains an explicit placeholder.
- Body inscriptions are 9.3 mm Georgia serif, 50% larger than v3's 6.2 mm. Exact wording: “before the crowds there was you” and “every step i took began beside you.” Both have hearts underneath.

## Review files

`Every-Step-Legacy-appearance-v4.blend` is editable. `appearance_v4.py` rebuilds it in local Blender. Ten actual model renders cover front, rear, side, left, top, perspective, upper detail, base detail, and front/rear source-style angles.

The broad black blade's front/rear/side-return boundaries are welded before smoothing; exposed texture is masked at the bronze boundary. Final rendering is checked for seams, clipping, cap appearance, inscription legibility and plaque visibility. Owner review is still necessary: matching a concept board requires interpretation, and no visual review substitutes for later engineering or physical samples.
