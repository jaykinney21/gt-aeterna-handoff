A Quality Control (QC) Checklist is your best defense against production errors. When you receive your first prototypes, you aren't just looking for beauty; you're looking for mechanical perfection and durability.
Here is a checklist tailored to your specific product requirements (200+ cubic inches, airtight seals, and complex textures).

Prototype Quality Control (QC) Checklist
1. Physical Dimensions & Volume
[ ] Internal Volume Test: Does the urn hold at least 200 cubic inches of dry material (e.g., rice or beads)?
[ ] Wall Thickness: Are the walls uniform? (Thin spots can lead to cracking over time).
[ ] Weight: Is the urn heavy enough to feel premium, but light enough to be handled easily?
2. Mechanical & Seal Integrity
[ ] Base Plate Fit: Does the plate sit flush with the bottom of the urn without wobbling?
[ ] Screw Alignment: Do the screws go in smoothly without stripping the threads?
[ ] Airtight Test: With the silicone gasket installed and the plate screwed down, is there any air leakage?
[ ] Adhesive Bond: If adhesive was used on any decorative elements (like the snake on the basketball urn), is it secure and invisible?
3. Aesthetic & Finish Quality
[ ] Color Accuracy: Do the colors match your "Mamba Purple," "Cheeto Orange," or "Piano Black" specs exactly?
[ ] Texture Fidelity: Is the "elephant print" or "snake scale" deep enough to be tactile, or did it get "washed out" during the glazing/casting process?
[ ] Typography/Graphics: Are the words (e.g., "STRAIGHT OUTTA COMPTON") sharp and centered? Check for bleeding or crooked letters.
[ ] Inlay Quality: On the "Kaleidoscope" urn, are the metallic inlays flush with the wood/resin, or can you feel a "lip"?
4. Material Durability
[ ] Stress Test: Does the finish chip if bumped lightly with a metal tool? (Simulating shipping/handling).
[ ] UV Resistance: (If possible) Does the pigment fade or yellow when placed in direct sunlight for 48 hours?
[ ] Cleaning: Does a damp microfiber cloth leave streaks or damage the matte/gloss finish?

Action Steps if a Prototype Fails:
Photograph the defect: Take high-resolution photos of the specific issue.
Reference the Spec List: Point out exactly which item in the Master Spec Document was not met.
Request a "Golden Sample": Do not approve mass production until you have one "Golden Sample" that passes every single check above.
