The spoken “200 cubic feet” is treated as **200 cubic inches**, approximately **3.28 liters**. The older blue-sneaker brief requested 210+ cubic inches; its display capacity should be reconciled rather than silently changed. Dimensions include the base, lid, feet, artwork projections and finish.

A larger **7-inch-diameter × 4-inch-high outside cylinder** has a gross envelope of only **153.94 in³**. A **7 × 7 × 4-inch outside square** has **196 in³**. Both miss 200 in³ before walls, closure and hardware. A rectangular base needs its depth specified. The 2–4-inch-high, roughly 7-inch-wide base aesthetic therefore needs a larger footprint, different height/proportions or a different storage route.

## New model and specification handoff

For each design, provide the original Tripo STL download and, if available, its GLB/textures, source project and actual input reference images. Include the latest specification and the intended whole-assembly height, width, depth and storage route. Keep larger display and compact revisions distinct.

We can then:

1. Preserve and inventory the original exports, identify their revision, units, component roles and artwork/spec matches.
2. Render front, back, both sides and top/base views; compare silhouette, detail, lettering and texture against the chosen references.
3. Inspect meshes for scale, disconnected parts, holes, self-intersections and thin features. Identify solid, decorative-hollow and genuinely connected storage geometry separately.
4. Reconcile the target usable capacity with the chamber, closure, mounting and liner. Explicitly separate measured values from intended values.
5. Write or complete the per-design quote sheet and component manifest. Ask vendors to price engineering gaps separately rather than pretending generated art is a released urn.
6. Send the selected **new revision** for comparable quotations, preserving artwork/base/closure/hardware/finishing/packaging/freight distinctions.
7. Choose one representative physical pilot after quotes and budget decisions. Validate filling, capacity, closure, stability, appearance and fit before production.

Tripo can export mesh formats including STL; format conversion is not mechanical CAD engineering. An STL does not carry a portable textured appearance. Keep an appearance export/reference alongside geometry and request editable engineering CAD for functional parts where it is commissioned. [Tripo format conversion documentation](https://developers.tripo3d.ai/en/docs/models-convert).

The detailed [new STL intake checklist](research/new-stl-intake-and-specification-checklist-2026-10-04.md) distinguishes a budgetary quote, firm manufacturing quote and production release. These are current design-intent drafts; they are not released manufacturing specifications.

## Material and cemetery evidence

SLA and MJF name printing processes; the quote needs an actual resin or nylon material. Sculpteo's published prototyping resin is described as UV sensitive and unsuitable for production/functional prototypes because of low impact strength. That finding applies to **that resin**, not every SLA material. Its published build dimensions also require supplier confirmation for these large artworks. Ask for a durable material and the total cost after splitting, support removal, washing/curing, surface preparation, assembly and finishing. [Sculpteo prototyping resin guide](https://pro.sculpteo.com/en/materials/stereolithography/prototyping-resin/).

No universal 10-inch acceptance rule was verified for Inglewood, Rose Hills and Forest Lawn. Forest Lawn's official 2023 two-capacity niche flyer emphasizes width under six inches for many such niches and checking the selected property. Confirm current **opening and interior width × depth × height**, material rules and insertion allowance for the exact intended niche. [Forest Lawn niche flyer](https://center.forestlawn.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/FL-230312-Urn-Niche-Capacity-Flyer-v4-0404_HiRes_TrimCURRENT.pdf).
