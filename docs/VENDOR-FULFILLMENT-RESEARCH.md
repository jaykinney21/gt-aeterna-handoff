## The commercial question

Made-to-order describes when manufacturing happens. Dropshipping describes who ships to the customer. We can combine them, but a supplier offering single prints and shipping is not automatically a complete branded fulfillment service.

Compare three models: (A) supplier makes, finishes, inspects, kits and ships each customer order; (B) supplier sends finished units to us or a fulfillment partner for inspection, accessories and customer shipping; (C) a small batch of approved standard units is stocked for fast dispatch, with personalization done separately if required. Model A minimizes finished inventory but must carry production time, individual finishing/setup costs and shipment risk. Models B/C add handling/storage but give more control. These are planning tradeoffs, not measured costs yet.

## Amazon implications

Amazon requires a third-party fulfillment arrangement to identify our business as the seller of record on packing slips, invoices and external packaging, and leaves customer-return responsibility with us. Supplier-neutral packaging alone is not enough: the full seller identification and returns arrangement matters. [Amazon drop shipping policy](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/201808410?locale=de-DE).

Personalized products offered through Amazon Custom use merchant fulfillment rather than FBA. Merely printing a standard design after purchase does not automatically make it an Amazon Custom product. Keep production/handling time separate from carrier transit, and validate actual seller-account settings before listing. [Amazon Custom](https://sell.amazon.com/programs/custom). Amazon's June 29, 2026 handling-time update calls for accurate SKU handling times and states exemptions for custom, handmade and Heavy & Bulky LTL shipments; do not assume our products qualify for an exemption. [Amazon announcement](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/da197a8b-e781-4530-99db-eb0ac8a5876d).

## Secondary candidates

These remain backups, not qualified fulfillment partners:

- **Sculpteo:** official API service supports uploads, pricing, ordering and production/shipping tracking. This makes it worth investigating for repeat-order automation, but does not establish branded packaging, urn/accessory kitting or Amazon integration. Its public shipping estimate is explicitly after production departure, not total order-to-delivery time. [API services](https://pro.sculpteo.com/en/services/api-services/), [service overview](https://pro.sculpteo.com/en/).
- **FacFox:** official knowledgebase says shipments originate from China and describes courier options and special handling for a customer's own courier account. Cross-border landed cost, lawful customs valuation, current duties and a no-surprise-charge delivery arrangement need written quotation; do not rely on the page's low declared-value examples. No complete branded customer-fulfillment commitment was established in this review. [Knowledgebase](https://facfox.com/docs/).
- **JLC3DP:** official help center provides shipment tracking, tariff, painting and refund guidance, but reviewed sources did not establish complete branded dropshipping or accessory kitting. Obtain parcel-specific terms; its sea-freight service for orders over 150 kg is not relevant to one-urn customer shipments. [Help center](https://jlc3dp.com/help), [sea-freight scope](https://jlc3dp.com/help/article/sea-shipment-guidelines).
- **PCBWay:** public shipping guide separates fabrication from transit and gives several international carrier choices. It is PCB-oriented, so do not treat it as confirmation of 3D urn fulfillment terms or use transit estimates as a total urn lead time. Ask its additive team for the actual process. [Shipping guide](https://www.pcbway.com/shipping_method_guide.aspx).

## Quote addendum — apply consistently to each shortlisted vendor

1. **Samples:** quote one finished assembly for each of the original five designs to [sample ZIP omitted]. Separate design correction, print, finish, hardware, assembly, inspection, protective packaging and freight. State whether combined shipment saves cost or delays completed samples.
2. **Repeat orders:** after design/sample approval, can you manufacture one unit per paid customer order without a batch minimum or recurring engineering fee? Quote repeat quantity-one price and separate 10/25/50 batch options. Monthly volume scenarios are forecasts only, not commitments.
3. **Brand identity:** can every packing slip, return label, invoice and external shipper identify our business appropriately, with no supplier marketing or retail price insert? Provide a sample packout and written seller-of-record arrangement.
4. **Full bundle:** can you receive, store, inspect and include externally sourced companion keepsakes/jewelry, instructions and memorial activation cards? Price receiving, storage, assembly/pick-pack and missing-item handling. An urn-printing quote does not include accessory manufacture or fulfillment unless expressly stated.
5. **Dispatch reliability:** state business days for repeat printing, hand finishing, assembly/QC and carrier handoff; cutoff times, peak-season changes, expedite costs and escalation if dispatch will be late. State carrier transit separately. Provide tracking at actual carrier handoff.
6. **Destination pricing:** quote sample freight to [sample ZIP omitted] separately from a lower-48 residential shipping rate model by zone or representative West/Central/East destinations. State packed dimensions/weight, insurance, dimensional-weight and residential/remote surcharges; exclude Alaska/Hawaii/international unless separately priced. No real customer addresses are needed yet.
7. **Quality:** retain an approved physical finish master and manufacturing revision; inspect cavity/closure, finish, personalization and kit completeness. Can you provide pre-shipment photos and an inspection record? Define remake, damage and missing-item evidence and response times.
8. **Returns:** who receives buyer returns, who pays for defect/damage remakes and freight, and what happens with non-defect or personalized returns? Our Amazon customer obligation may be broader than the supplier's manufacturing warranty. Do not promise customer refunds are covered by the vendor.
9. **Order transfer:** start with a controlled manual order/PO workflow; ask about CSV/API later. Capture customer address, SKU/revision, personalization approval, promised ship date and tracking without building an integration prematurely. Confirm customer data is used only for fulfillment.
10. **Economics:** calculate contribution from selling price less print/finish/hardware, accessories, packout/fulfillment, shipping, marketplace fees, advertising and a returns/remake allowance. Amortize engineering/sample costs separately. No price or margin is known until written quotes arrive.

## Recommended qualification test

Keep the five original designs in the RFQ. After evaluating physical samples at [sample ZIP omitted], select one approved design for a repeat-order fulfillment trial: correct brand packing, complete bundle, inspection evidence, dispatch tracking and safe arrival. Only then choose between direct supplier fulfillment and local/third-party kitting. Do not promise a 5–7-day customer delivery window from a generic printing lead time.

Public shipping policies establish capabilities, not acceptance of this particular urn project. Written supplier responses remain required for costs, finish fidelity and repeat-order service commitments.
