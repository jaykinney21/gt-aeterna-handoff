# Cemetery fit research — October 4, 2026

**Decision:** Keep 10 inches as the proposed assembled-height target for the compact variant. Do not label it approved for Inglewood Park Cemetery, Rose Hills or Forest Lawn until the relevant cemetery confirms the exact property's usable dimensions and material/placement rules. A height target alone does not establish fit.

This is an official-source desk review. No cemetery was contacted. The mother's prior research is useful input, but a public universal 10-inch policy was not verified in this review.

## Current storage clarification — supersedes the initial base-only assumption

The latest owner direction distinguishes the sizes. The **compact variant must retain 200 in³ of usable ash capacity within a maximum 10-inch finished assembly**. It may use a full hollow vessel, such as a vase with an integrated foot, or a deliberately connected chamber through a hollow upper and short base. A separate shallow base does **not** have to hold the entire compact capacity. The larger display version may retain decorative artwork above a separate storage base, provided that base's capacity is established. See the [current modular manufacturing clarification](modular-manufacturing-2026-10-04.md).

The recommended compact route is an engineered full hollow vessel with an integrated foot or minimal pedestal, with the artwork shaped around its reservoir where needed. This is subject to the specific niche's width, depth, height, clear opening and material rules. For a connected upper/base alternative, every counted volume must be accessible through the fill route and remain sealed at the joining interface. Disconnected decorative voids cannot be added to the storage-capacity total.

## Questions needed before compact CAD dimensions are frozen

For each target cemetery and exact niche/property type, obtain written answers to:

1. What is the maximum permitted **assembled exterior width × depth × height**, including base, artwork, plaque, feet, lid and every projection? Is the limit 10 inches tall specifically, and is that a hard maximum or an approximate recommendation?
2. What are the minimum usable internal dimensions and the clear opening after the front/door is removed? Are there frame lips, door hardware, shelves or tapered interiors that reduce insertion space?
3. Is the niche single or shared? What space is allocated to this urn after existing urns and mementos are considered, and must space be preserved for a future second urn?
4. What installation/handling clearance is required? Is the cemetery willing to approve a dimensioned drawing and a physical fit model before production?
5. Are painted SLA resin, PA12 nylon or other proposed plastics permitted? Are there durability, finish, closure, identification, liner or weight requirements?
6. May an integrated hollow vessel or a two-part sculpture/base be placed as an assembly? If a connected upper/base chamber is chosen, are there additional assembly or closure requirements? For a separate base-only display route, may the ash-containing base be placed alone with the artwork kept at home, and would the assembly need permanent fastening?
7. If the property is outdoor or exposed, what moisture, temperature or sunlight conditions and material restrictions apply? Are additional containers required for an in-ground placement?

## Practical design requirements — proposed, not cemetery policy

- Define two **assembled** product heights: compact target ≤10 inches (254 mm) and home-display target about 12 inches (304.8 mm), pending final design decisions. Each includes the complete storage vessel, artwork and any foot, base, lid or projection; “10-inch artwork plus base” would exceed the compact target.
- Specify W × D separately for every base shape and sculpture. A 10-inch-tall figure with a wide circular base can fail a shared niche's width allowance.
- Reserve room for print tolerance, paint thickness and cemetery-requested insertion clearance. Do not make a nominal 10-inch model exactly 10 inches if the confirmed opening is also exactly 10 inches; agree the margin with the manufacturer and cemetery.
- Keep required usable ash capacity independent of visual scale. For the compact route, measure the complete accessible sealed vessel or connected upper/base chamber; for a base-only display route, measure only the base's chamber. Subtract wall thickness, closure intrusion, screw bosses, gasket ledges and mounting features. Outer bounding-box volume is not usable capacity.
- Use the same verified base/interface across compatible larger display artworks where practical. A standard interface can reduce redesign, but rectangular, square and circular exteriors do not automatically provide the same internal volume or fit. Compact vessels may require different architecture and should not be forced onto a shallow display base.
- For a base-only display route, keep artwork attachment mechanically separate from the sealed ash chamber, or explicitly engineer/test any penetration. For a compact shared chamber, deliberately engineer the communicating passage and ash-retaining interface; removing either component can open the storage chamber. A detachable decorative cover around an independently sealed vessel offers a separate design option.
- Treat the 12-inch home variant as a display product with its own capacity, stability and closure tests. It is not automatically prohibited or accepted for cemetery use; that depends on the actual property.

The compact route is now a full hollow vessel or connected upper/base chamber. For the current compact direction, design the hollow vessel, artwork silhouette, footprint, closure and 200 in³ of accessible volume together. An integrated foot or short pedestal can preserve more vessel height than a separate storage base. A connected hollow figurine/base may also work, but passage access, sealability, process-specific walls and usable capacity need independent verification. Uniformly shrinking an older adult urn does not preserve its capacity. The larger display/base-only design remains a conditional option: its proposed pedestal dimensions must actually deliver the selected capacity after all deductions. Do not quietly lower capacity to achieve a visual design.

## Fit and release test

1. Record the cemetery, park, building/section, niche type/identifier, date and written envelope/material approval in the design specification.
2. Compare the finished assembly's measured maximum W × D × H and intended insertion orientation against both the approved internal envelope and opening. Confirm allowance for adjacent/future urns.
3. Create a low-cost full-size fit model or rigid bounding-envelope fixture, including all projections, and seek the cemetery's fit confirmation.
4. Verify the actual CAD chamber volume and perform a physical capacity/closure test with an appropriate non-remains surrogate. For the compact route, confirm at least 200 in³ of accessible usable volume in the full vessel or connected upper/base chamber after closure and hardware deductions; do not count unreachable or disconnected voids. For the larger base-only route, confirm the selected capacity in the base alone. Verify that the fill opening works for the intended liner and that the closed chamber, including any communicating assembly seam, retains the surrogate through handling.
5. Check the filled assembly's stability, attachment security and repeatable closure; inspect a production-process sample after finishing because finishing changes dimensions and surface behavior.
6. Release cemetery-specific compatibility claims only for the configurations actually approved. “Compact 10-inch design; confirm your niche dimensions” is an honest interim description.
