## Recommended starting scope

One original urn, one size, one material/finish, and one reliable closure. Gmail recovery added **Midnight Blossom**, a hollow floral body with a separate threaded lid. Evaluate this existing model first as a possible prototype candidate. It is top-loading, which conflicts with the established bottom-loading design intent; accept an explicit first-SKU exception or commission a redesign before quoting. Avoid launching six colors or a fully bespoke configurator before one physical sample passes. See [GMAIL-DRIVE-TRIAGE.md](GMAIL-DRIVE-TRIAGE.md).

Priscilla's January master brief and later specifications repeatedly establish **at least 200 cubic inches** for an adult urn and concealed bottom access. Use 200 in³ (approximately 3.277 liters) as the inherited minimum requirement, measured after the lid and internal features. No recovered model has a verified usable-capacity result. Freeze the selected SKU's target and any margin above that minimum; a keepsake or pet SKU would be a separate scope decision.

## Engineering deliverables

- Editable native CAD, a dimensioned STEP solid model for body and closure, and print exports (STL or 3MF) with units explicitly stated in the accompanying drawing.
- Section views proving a hollow interior, specified wall and base thickness, opening clearance, closure interfaces, and capacity after all intrusions.
- Assembly drawing; tolerances agreed with the selected manufacturer; hardware/liner/seal bill of materials where applicable.
- Material and finish specifications, cosmetic inspection areas, stable support/base design, and packaging concept.
- A revision identifier tying the drawing, mesh, photos, and quote to the same product.

A printable mesh can be sufficient for additive quotes; STEP is especially useful as an editable engineering handoff. Xometry accepts STEP and mesh formats including STL/3MF, and Protolabs recommends STEP for uploads while also accepting STL. File acceptance alone does not validate the part. [Xometry file guidance](https://community.xometry.com/kb/articles/643-what-file-types-does-xometry-accept), [Protolabs 3D printing](https://www.protolabs.com/services/3d-printing/).
